# Audit — CCOC boundary against CREST and MLTR

## Purpose

CCOC sits between two neighbouring dissertation chapters and is vulnerable to being read as either a corollary of CREST or a special case of MLTR. This audit fixes the quantified objects and ownership boundaries.

## 1. CCOC versus CREST

| Dimension | CREST Chapter 1 | CCOC Chapter 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Primary change | management capability / admissible control changes | legal future-response grammar opens |
| Physical plant | declared finite controlled system | one fixed controlled plant |
| Main comparison | carrier gain versus state/evidence burden | open exact interface versus independently optimized closed interfaces |
| Headline quantity | \(\Delta|K^*|=1,\ \Delta K_{U_0}=m\) | \(|P_C|=2,\ |P_O|=2^{m+1},\ K_O-K_C=m\) |
| Evidence layer | explicit evidence partition and monitoring debt | not a headline object |
| Target reportability | explicit yes/no separation | not a headline object |
| Source owner | `crest` | `ccoc` |

### Allowed bridge

Future addressability is a structural explanation for how one newly relevant action or connection can reveal distinctions hidden by an earlier representation.

### Forbidden upgrade

Do not state that CCOC proves the CREST capability–resolution theorem. CCOC does not compute robust carrier gain, evidence debt, or target reportability. Do not state that CREST subsumes the CCOC cross-grammar minimum-interface comparison; its theorem uses different quantified objects.

## 2. CCOC versus MLTR

| Dimension | CCOC Chapter 2 | MLTR Chapter 3 |
|---|---|---|
| System relation | same controlled plant; grammar changes | source and target systems may be non-nested |
| Initial macrostate | independently optimized for each grammar | one inherited source partition is fixed |
| Optimization | compare closed minima with open minimum | minimize target repair subject to preserving carried source semantics |
| Headline failure | closed compression does not survive open grammar | inherited macro-law does not survive replacement |
| Repair | positive common-schema sufficient boundary | unique coarsest source-relative target repair |
| History | not owned | route coherence and minimal history augmentation owned |
| Source owner | `ccoc` | `mltr` |

### Allowed transition

A replacement may also introduce new legal futures, so an MLTR example can contain a CCOC-like forced split. The chapter transition is legitimate when the question changes from unconstrained minimum interface size to preservation of one inherited source law.

### Forbidden collapse

Do not call MLTR's transport defect the CCOC gap. Do not import path/history completion into CCOC. Do not treat independently optimized closed interfaces as inherited labels.

## 3. Prior-art firewall

The following are substrate or ancestry, not standalone CCOC novelties:

- continuation-based response equivalence;
- fixed-grammar minimization;
- input/specification-dependent reduction;
- pair-separation cardinality arguments;
- generic compression/composition noncommutation;
- bounded-local or modular machine realization in isolation.

The chapter-level contribution remains the exact quantitative fixed-plant package with one newly legal primitive action, maximum \(m\)-bit response-interface increase, bounded local structure, and logarithmic access.

## 4. Dissertation wording lock

Preferred:

> CCOC quantifies how exact interfaces optimized under restricted future grammars can fail to combine into one comparably small interface after those futures are opened. The result is distinct from CREST's carrier/evidence scale separation and from MLTR's source-relative repair after system replacement.

Avoid:

> All three chapters prove that state depends on context.

The latter is true at a slogan level but destroys the separate novelty of each chapter.

## 5. Transition clauses

### Chapter 1 → Chapter 2

> The conservation theorem shows that one new capability can create arbitrary state and monitoring debt. CCOC isolates the future-addressability structure that allows many dormant distinctions to become simultaneously response-relevant.

### Chapter 2 → Chapter 3

> CCOC keeps the controlled plant fixed while changing legal futures. MLTR asks what happens when the source system itself is replaced and an inherited macro-law must be carried rather than re-optimized from scratch.

## Claim ceiling

This is an editorial and type audit. It does not establish historical priority, empirical ecological application, or a new bridge theorem among the three programmes. Source claims remain governed by their owning repositories.
