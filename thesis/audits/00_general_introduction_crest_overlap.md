# Overlap audit — General Introduction versus CREST Chapter 1

## Decision

The General Introduction and the CREST chapter answer different questions.

- **General Introduction:** When may a representation earned for one ecological responsibility be reused for another?
- **CREST chapter:** When does an expansion of conservation capability force a finer ecological state and monitoring requirement?

The General Introduction owns the cross-chapter reuse problem, minimal factorization language, universe-wide invariants, bridge discipline, and dissertation roadmap. The CREST chapter owns the conservation paradox, the full contract-relative state construction, well-posed-contract criteria, shallow-lake worked case, capability–resolution theorem, evidence gates, and conservation implications.

3. does not introduce the full CREST contract tuple;
4. does not define temporally extended worlds in CREST-specific form;
5. does not reproduce the shallow-lake worked case;

9. makes bridge conditions and directional reuse, rather than ecological state definition, the dissertation-level contribution.

## Required Chapter 1 adaptation

When the CREST paper is converted into Chapter 1:

- shorten its philosophical review of abstraction, idealization, multiple realization, and adequacy-for-purpose;
- point readers to the General Introduction for the generic reuse problem;
- retain the ecological-state question because Chapter 1 needs it to define the capability result;
- retain the full state/evidence/target formalization, shallow-lake case, theorem, and proof;
- open with the conservation paradox rather than reintroducing the entire dissertation architecture;
- end by asking how one new action can expose so many formerly merged differences, providing the transition to CCOC.

## Claim ceiling

This audit is editorial. It does not establish that the cited philosophical literatures lack other relevant transport accounts, and it does not change the novelty or evidence status of CREST. Final prose still requires bibliography-level checking against primary publications and the target institution's dissertation format.
