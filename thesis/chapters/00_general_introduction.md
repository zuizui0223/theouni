# General Introduction — The Reuse Problem in Ecology

**Status:** source-bounded writing brief  
**Primary owner:** `theouni` synthesis only

## Problem

Ecology routinely reuses state labels, reduced laws, monitoring variables, causal interpretations, and warning indicators beyond the task or domain for which they were first built. The introduction asks when that reuse is scientifically licensed.

Use only the minimum common formal language:

\[
R\models\alpha
\iff
\Sigma_\alpha=f\circ R.
\]

A representation is adequate for task `alpha` when the task-complete response can be reconstructed from what was retained.

## Headline result

The dissertation is a theory of transport and non-transport of scientific representations across changed ecological responsibilities, not a catalogue of competing ecological-state definitions.

## Why the result is nontrivial

Purpose relativity, abstraction, hidden mechanisms, historical contingency, and evidence limits are individually familiar. The dissertation earns coherence only by showing distinct, source-owned conditions under which reuse fails, succeeds, or requires repair.

## Ecological payoff

The framing turns a broad state debate into auditable questions: What changed? Which response must still factor through the retained representation? Which bridge is required before a previous result can travel?

## Claim ceiling

Do not present factorization, quotient construction, purpose relativity, or the Worldline Atlas itself as the principal theorem novelty. Do not imply that task choice creates ecological reality or that one intrinsic ecological state exists.
