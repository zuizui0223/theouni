# Chapter 3 — When Macro-Laws Do Not Survive Ecological Replacement

**Status:** source-bounded writing brief  
**Primary owner:** `zuizui0223/mltr`

## Problem

Species turnover, extinction, recolonization, habitat reconfiguration, and rewiring may replace one ecological system with a non-nested target system. The chapter asks whether an accepted source macro-law can be carried across that change without losing its inherited operational meaning.

## Headline result

MLTR gives exact transport conditions, finite fiber-split obstructions, the unique coarsest target repair constrained to preserve every valid inherited source merge, a transport-defect measure, path-label coherence, and minimal history augmentation when replacement routes carry different terminal meanings.

## Why the result is nontrivial

The target representation is not optimized from scratch. Its minimality is source-relative: repair must preserve inherited semantics wherever they remain exact. This distinguishes the problem from ordinary target-only aggregation and from CCOC's independently optimized closed/open comparison.

## Ecological payoff

A state label or reduced law used before turnover may cease to support the same management interpretation afterward. The chapter identifies when transport is exact and the least additional state or history context needed when it is not.

## Claim ceiling

Do not claim that the replacement relation or history is inferred from field data, that the repaired state is automatically future-sufficient under every grammar, or that it is empirically identified by current observations.
