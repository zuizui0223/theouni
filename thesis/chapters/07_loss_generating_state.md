# Chapter 7 — Which State Actually Generates Functional Loss?

**Status:** source-bounded writing brief  
**Primary owner:** `zuizui0223/eco-genetic-criticality`  
**Embedded firewall:** TU-3

## Problem

Early-warning analyses are meaningless until the downstream loss process has been defined independently. This chapter asks which representation is sufficient for the declared eco-genetic functional-loss response and which common summaries erase transition-relevant structure.

## Headline result

The complete explicit simulator state is future-sufficient under the declared Markov closure, but two states can match census, interaction and allele-frequency marginals, realised trait-bin state, \(H_\alpha\), \(H_\gamma\), and \(F_{ST}\) while differing in patchwise cross-layer alignment and therefore in the next interaction transition. TU-3 separates raw simulator detail from the contract-complete loss-generating quotient.

## Why the result is nontrivial

The chapter avoids both extremes: the full simulator state is sufficient but not thereby minimal or natural, while familiar coarse marginals can be demonstrably insufficient. State adequacy depends on the complete loss response, not on detail count alone.

## Ecological payoff

Functional loss, warning, and portability tests must be conditioned on a representation that preserves the relevant joint spatial process rather than on convenient marginal indicators or habitat labels.

## Claim ceiling

Do not claim a universal alignment-risk rule, natural-state minimality, universal connectivity effect, or directional long-horizon loss result from the bounded alignment witness. Complete simulator sufficiency remains model-closure conditional.
