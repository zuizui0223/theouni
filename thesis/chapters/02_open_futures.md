# Chapter 2 — When Closed Simplicity Fails under Open Futures

**Status:** source-bounded writing brief  
**Primary owner:** `zuizui0223/ccoc`

## Problem

A model may admit a small exact interface under every fixed closed composition or response grammar. The chapter asks whether those individually simple interfaces imply a comparably simple interface once the legal future grammar is opened.

## Headline result

The CCOC family has a constant-sized closed interface but an open exact interface of size \(2^{m+1}\), producing an \(m\)-bit extension/compression gap. Constrained codebooks and a bounded-local relay show that the gap is not limited to a full Cartesian lookup table.

## Why the result is nontrivial

Fixed-grammar minimization and distinguishability are classical substrate. The chapter earns novelty from the quantified cross-grammar comparison: separately optimized closed representations do not compose into a small exact open representation.

## Ecological payoff

Compression calibrated under fixed communities, fixed connectivity, or fixed intervention sets may become inadequate when colonization, reconnection, rewiring, or new interventions become legally addressable futures.

## Claim ceiling

Do not claim novelty for Myhill–Nerode minimization, generic state blow-up, partition refinement, or modular sequential-machine realization. Do not infer observed-ecosystem validation from finite certificates.
