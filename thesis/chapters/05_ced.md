## Chapter thesis

An experiment justifies a deterministic ecological report only when the requested target is constant on the reliability-qualified class of worlds compatible with the record. More data, finer nominal records, or larger entropy reduction can leave the required prediction unresolved.

## Forbidden inference

\[
\boxed{
	ext{more, finer, or nominally distinguishing information}
\not\Rightarrow
	ext{a licensed ecological target}
}
\]

## Source-owned result chain

1. A finite experiment induces an exact compatible-world quotient.
2. Deterministic reporting is justified exactly when the target is constant on the compatible class; otherwise the sharp report retains ambiguity.
3. A unique coarsest target/action-safe refinement specifies the additional resolution that would be sufficient, without pretending the current record has earned it.
4. Imperfect observation and shared failure domains constrain which nominal splits may be credited.
5. Adaptive policies are judged by correct resolution, wrong resolution, honest ambiguity, and cost under an explicit false-resolution contract.

## Central contrast

Repeated effort within one shared failure domain is not equivalent to independent observation modes. Likewise, full-world information gain can prefer target-irrelevant detail over the experiment that actually resolves the declared prediction.

## Prohibited upgrades

- The target-safe refinement has already been observed.
- Arbitrarily many negative repeats prove absence under imperfect sensitivity.
- More latent-world information is automatically more predictive.
- Full latent-world identification is always the scientific objective.
- A nominal record split is trustworthy without a reliability contract.
