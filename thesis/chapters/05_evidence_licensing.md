# Chapter 5 — When Evidence Does Not License the State We Need

**Status:** source-bounded writing brief  
**Primary owner:** `zuizui0223/ced`

## Problem

A task may require ecological distinctions that a finite experiment nominally or reliably fails to resolve. The chapter asks what the observation record actually identifies, what target can be reported, and when additional resolution may be credited under detection failure and error risk.

## Headline result

Experiment records induce exact compatible-world classes. A deterministic target report is justified only when the target is constant on the compatible class. The least target-safe resolution requirement is distinct from current identification, and trustworthy refinement depends on failure architecture and an explicit false-resolution contract.

## Why the result is nontrivial

The chapter couples target-relative reporting to observation reliability. More entropy reduction can resolve target-irrelevant details, while repeated sampling inside one shared failure domain cannot substitute automatically for independent observation modes.

## Ecological payoff

Monitoring can stop with licensed ignorance when the requested target is already constant, or retain honest ambiguity when it is not. The goal is defensible target resolution rather than full latent-world identification.

## Claim ceiling

Do not claim that a required target-safe refinement has already been empirically identified, that nominal record separation is reliable, or that finite exact results automatically extend to continuous natural-world support.
