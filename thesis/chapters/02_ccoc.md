## Chapter thesis

Exact interfaces optimized separately under fixed closed future grammars need not provide a comparably small exact interface when the admissible future grammar is opened.

## Forbidden inference

\[
\boxed{
\text{simple exact interfaces under each closed grammar}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{simple exact interface under the open grammar}
}
\]

## Source-owned result

The canonical family has

\[
|P_C|=2,
\qquad
|P_O|=2^{m+1},
\qquad
K_O-K_C=m.
\]

The manuscript spine is the cross-grammar extension/compression lower bound, with constrained-codebook and bounded-local relay results used as robustness and sharpness support. Fixed-grammar quotient construction and generic distinguishability are acknowledged classical substrate.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

The chapter must not claim novelty for Myhill–Nerode-style equivalence, fixed-input minimization, incomplete-machine reduction, generic modular realization, or the fact that one input can distinguish states. The defensible claim is the quantified failure of exact fixed-composition compression to commute with opening the declared response grammar, together with an explicit constrained extremal witness.
