# 観測が原理的に届かない範囲

*English working title: Where Observation Cannot Reach in Principle*

> **Draft status:** theorem-refreshed source-bounded v0.1 from Boundary snapshot `2919842f19bdd93221363b9f39f2ba1ebb146d17`. The chapter owns an exact identification condition for a declared positive multiplicative/log-linear observation class. It does not claim that biologically richer measurements are generally uninformative, nor that structural non-identification establishes anything about whether the latent mechanism exists in nature.

## 1. The real question is not how much was measured, but whether the observation map changed

Ecology often treats a measurement as more mechanistic when it lies closer to biological machinery. Physiological rates, genomic states, molecular interactions, process-specific experiments and direct behavioural measurements can indeed reveal structures that coarse endpoint observations do not. But biological proximity and mechanism identification answer different questions.

Biological proximity asks where a measurement sits in the causal or functional chain. Identification asks whether the observed quantities distinguish the competing mechanisms that the scientific claim needs to separate. A proximal measurement can remain compatible with several mechanisms. Conversely, a simple field measurement can be strongly identifying when the declared alternatives make different predictions for it.

The forbidden inference of this chapter is therefore:

> **観測を豊かにした ⇒ 潜在機構に近づいた**

That sentence is only a motivation. By itself it would be close to a truism. The scientific question is sharper:

> **Given what has already been measured, under what exact condition does one additional observation reduce structural mechanism ambiguity?**

The answer is not “when the measurement is more detailed,” “when it is closer to the mechanism,” or even “when it is a different variable.” For the declared log-linear observation class, the condition is algebraic and necessary-and-sufficient: the new observation must add a direction that is not already generated by the current observation operator.

This distinction matters in data-rich ecological studies. A field programme may quantify plant phenotype, visitor identity, community composition, interaction frequency, microclimate and reproductive output and still miss the observation needed to separate the mechanisms under consideration. More records can improve precision along observed directions while leaving a missing mechanism direction untouched. The appropriate result in that case is not a post-hoc mechanistic label. It is an explicit statement of what remains non-identified and what measurement would change that fact.

## 2. Put the mechanism problem in log coordinates

Boundary studies a recurring ecological architecture in which positive latent stages combine multiplicatively. Suppose the declared latent channels are

\[
F_1,\ldots,F_k>0.
\]

Write their log values as

\[
x=(\log F_1,\ldots,\log F_k)^\top\in\mathbb R^k.
\]

Many exact multiplicative observations become linear in `x`. A product such as

\[
W=\prod_{j=1}^k F_j
\]

becomes

\[
\log W=(1,\ldots,1)x.
\]

A direct channel measurement `F_i` becomes the coordinate observation `e_i^T x`. A ratio of declared channel products becomes another linear row. More generally, let the current exact observation system be

\[
Mx=y,
\]

where each row of `M` describes one exact log-linear observation direction and `y` is the compatible observed value.

The model class is deliberately bounded. It does not assert that every ecological mechanism is multiplicative or log-linear. It asks a precise question for studies that already use this measurement architecture: once the observation map has been declared, what mechanism distinctions can it identify?

The key object is the set of latent channel states compatible with the observed record,

\[
\mathcal C(M,y)=\{x\in\mathbb R^k:Mx=y\}.
\]

If this set contains more than one point, the observation map has not point-identified all latent channels. What matters now is its dimension and how that dimension changes when another measurement is added.

## 3. Main theorem: compatible-set dimension is controlled exactly by observation rank

Assume `Mx=y` is compatible, and choose one compatible point `x_0`. Every other compatible point can be written as

\[
x=x_0+v
\]

with

\[
Mv=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal C(M,y)=x_0+\ker M.
\]

The compatible set is an affine translate of the null space. Translation does not change dimension, so

\[
\dim\mathcal C(M,y)=\dim\ker M.
\]

By rank-nullity,

\[
\boxed{\dim\mathcal C(M,y)=k-\operatorname{rank}(M).}
\]

This is the first theorem-level answer to the chapter question. Structural mechanism ambiguity is not a function of the number of files, observations, variables or replicates. In this declared exact class, it is the null dimension left by the observation operator.

Point identification occurs exactly when

\[
\operatorname{rank}(M)=k.
\]

If rank is smaller than `k`, some nonzero direction remains in `ker M`. Moving along that direction changes the latent channels without changing any currently declared exact observation.

This theorem also distinguishes precision from structure. A measurement can be estimated with smaller standard error, more replicates or finer numerical resolution without changing the row space of `M`. Such improvements matter statistically, but they do not change the exact structural dimension above. Structural identification changes only when the observation operator gains independent rank.

## 4. When does one additional measurement help? An if-and-only-if design condition

Now add one candidate scalar observation with row vector `a^T` and compatible value `b`. The augmented observation matrix is

\[
M^+=\begin{pmatrix}M\\a^T\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The new compatible-set dimension is

\[
k-\operatorname{rank}(M^+).
\]

Appending one row can increase matrix rank by at most one. The rank increases exactly when `a^T` is not already in the row span of `M`. Therefore:

> **Observation-rank theorem.** A new exact scalar observation reduces structural mechanism ambiguity **if and only if** its observation row lies outside the current row span. When it helps, it reduces the residual structural dimension by exactly one.

The proof supplies both directions.

**Necessity.** If `a^T` lies in the row span of `M`, then it is a linear combination of existing observation rows. The augmented matrix has the same rank as `M`, so the compatible-set dimension is unchanged. The observation may be biologically novel or measured by a different instrument, but it adds no new exact structural direction.

**Sufficiency.** If `a^T` lies outside the row span, appending it raises rank by one. Rank-nullity then lowers the compatible-set dimension by one. No extra interpretive assumption is needed within the declared class.

This theorem converts a vague recommendation—collect more mechanistic data—into a design test. Before measuring a candidate quantity, ask whether its declared observation map cuts the current equivalence class in a genuinely new direction.

Several practical consequences follow immediately.

First, exact repetition of an existing observation cannot improve structural identification. Its new row duplicates an existing row.

Second, rescaling an existing observation cannot improve structural identification. Multiplying a row by a nonzero constant does not change row span.

Third, a measurement that is a linear combination of already observed quantities adds no structural rank, even if the derived variable has a new biological name.

Fourth, higher numerical precision can reduce sampling uncertainty without changing structural rank when it leaves the observation operator unchanged.

Fifth, a biologically less proximal measurement can be more identifying than a proximal one if the former lies outside the current row span and the latter does not.

The contribution is therefore not the statement “more data are not always better.” It is the exact condition identifying when an additional observation changes the structural inferential problem.

## 5. The familiar `k-1-r` result is a corollary, not the general theorem

The earlier Boundary formulation emphasized a positive `k`-channel product

\[
W=\prod_{j=1}^kF_j.
\]

Endpoint-only observation contributes the log row

\[
(1,1,\ldots,1).
\]

This row has rank one, so the general theorem gives residual dimension

\[
k-1.
\]

Now directly observe `r` distinct channels. Their coordinate rows are `e_{i_1},...,e_{i_r}`. For `0\le r\le k-1`, these coordinate rows together with the all-ones product row are linearly independent. Hence the augmented rank is

\[
r+1,
\]

and the residual dimension is

\[
\boxed{k-1-r}.
\]

When `r=k-1`, the final channel is recovered from the net product and the observed channels. Point identification follows.

This derivation matters because it corrects a possible over-reading of the old formula. The important condition is not the *number* of anchors. The important condition is independence relative to the existing observation span. If two proposed “anchors” encode the same structural direction, counting both as independent would overstate identification.

The product result is therefore a transparent ecological special case of a more general measurement-design theorem. It remains useful because many ecological service chains have exactly this quantity-by-quality or rate-by-effectiveness form, but it is no longer the conceptual endpoint of the chapter.

## 6. A data-rich field programme can still hit a joint-measurement bottleneck

Pollination provides a concrete interpretation. For visitor type `m`, effective contribution may be represented as

\[
S_m=V_mE_m,
\]

where `V_m` is interaction quantity such as visitation rate and `E_m` is per-interaction effectiveness. Community service aggregates these contributions. Further downstream, realised reproduction may depend on pollen limitation, reproductive dependency, autonomous selfing or other processes.

Visitor identity is not effective service. Interaction quantity is not per-visit effectiveness. Effective service is not reproductive dependency. A final reproductive contrast does not reveal which unobserved intermediate changed.

Suppose a field programme measures community composition and visitation repeatedly but never measures effectiveness. More repeated visitation observations can estimate `V_m` increasingly well while leaving the `E_m` direction structurally unresolved. In the rank language, effort has increased without adding the missing independent observation row.

Likewise, adding another index computed from the same visitation records may not help if its exact observation row lies in the span of what is already measured. The field design becomes mechanistically informative only when a new measurement cuts a direction that the existing record did not constrain.

This is the joint-measurement bottleneck: a study can be rich in observations but poor in identifying directions. The correct output when the declared mechanisms remain inseparable is `not evaluable`, partial identification, or an explicit compatible set—not assignment of mechanism from proximity or narrative plausibility.

## 7. Structural rank is not the only identification problem: proxies introduce calibration transport

Even when a channel direction is nominally observed through a proxy, a second identification problem can remain. Consider two regimes `i in {0,1}` with

\[
W_i=F_iE_i,
\qquad
X_i=q_iF_i,
\]

where `X` is a proxy for channel `F` and `q_i` is the proxy conversion. Define

\[
\rho_W=W_1/W_0,
\quad
\rho_X=X_1/X_0,
\quad
\rho_F=F_1/F_0,
\quad
\rho_E=E_1/E_0,
\quad
\kappa=q_1/q_0.
\]

Then

\[
\rho_F=\rho_X/\kappa,
\qquad
\rho_E=(\rho_W/\rho_X)\kappa.
\]

Taking a ratio does not remove calibration change. Stable calibration is the condition `kappa=1`; it is not implied by relative comparison.

Boundary therefore declares a symmetric transport bound

\[
1/\Gamma\le\kappa\le\Gamma,
\qquad \Gamma\ge1.
\]

Conditional on positive observed `rho_W` and `rho_X`, the sharp joint identified set is

\[
J_\Gamma=
\{(\rho_X/\kappa,\widehat\rho_E\kappa):1/\Gamma\le\kappa\le\Gamma\},
\]

where `\widehat\rho_E=\rho_W/\rho_X` is the stable-calibration value.

This yields one continuous identification family:

- `Gamma=1`: point identification under stable calibration;
- finite `Gamma>1`: sharp partial identification;
- unrestricted transport: non-identification.

The same `kappa` moves the two channel ratios in opposite directions while preserving their product. The uncertainty object is therefore a coupled joint set, not a Cartesian product of marginal intervals.

The rank theorem and calibration theorem solve different problems. Rank asks whether the declared exact observations provide independent structural directions. Calibration transport asks whether the map from a proxy to the latent channel is stable enough across regimes to assign values within those directions. A design can solve one problem and still fail the other.

## 8. Breakdown factors make a hidden assumption testable as sensitivity

A finite `Gamma` is generally not identified from the same endpoint and proxy observations whose identifying power is under study. It must come from external calibration, validation, instrument knowledge or an explicit sensitivity contract.

For a stable-calibration estimate `\widehat\rho`, Boundary defines the smallest symmetric multiplicative distortion needed to reach no change as

\[
\Gamma^*=\max(\widehat\rho,1/\widehat\rho),
\qquad
\eta^*=|\log\widehat\rho|.
\]

This breakpoint is invariant to reversing the reference regime. Rather than choose one convenient calibration tolerance after seeing the desired conclusion, the analyst reports the smallest transport deviation sufficient to overturn the directional claim.

This is an example of partial identification used positively. The conclusion is not “mechanism cannot be known.” It is: under the declared proxy model, this range is identified, this assumption controls its width, and this exact distortion is sufficient to erase the directional conclusion.

## 9. Channel anchors and calibration anchors solve different missing-information problems

Boundary distinguishes two types of anchor.

**Channel anchors** measure latent ecological stages directly. Their identifying value is governed by rank. A channel anchor helps if its observation row adds a new independent direction.

**Calibration anchors** measure the proxy conversion. With zero direct calibration anchors, cross-regime conversion may be unrestricted unless externally bounded. One regime-specific calibration identifies conversion locally but does not determine cross-regime drift. Two regime-specific calibrations identify both `q_0` and `q_1`, hence `kappa=q_1/q_0` for the comparison.

The two ladders should not be conflated. A precise calibration measurement can remove proxy uncertainty without adding a new ecological channel direction. A direct ecological channel measurement can increase structural rank even if a separate calibration problem remains for other proxies.

The operational rule is therefore conditional rather than rhetorical: diagnose which information is missing, and collect the observation that changes that specific identification condition.

## 10. What has actually been proved

The chapter establishes the following results under declared observation models.

1. For compatible exact log-linear observations `Mx=y`, the compatible latent-channel set is an affine translate of `ker M` and has dimension `k-rank(M)`.
2. Point identification occurs exactly at full column rank.
3. One additional scalar observation reduces structural ambiguity **if and only if** its row lies outside the current row span; when useful it reduces dimension by exactly one.
4. Repetition, rescaling and exact linear combinations of existing observations do not change structural rank.
5. The positive product result `k-1-r` follows as a special case when the endpoint product row is combined with `r` independent coordinate anchors.
6. Proxy calibration transport yields a sharp point/partial/non-identification family under a declared symmetric bound.
7. The calibration-induced uncertainty is coupled, and a reference-invariant breakdown factor identifies the assumption strength needed to overturn a directional conclusion.

The source implementation verifies the rank theorem with exact rational arithmetic and an independent determinant/minor oracle over small integer matrices. It exhaustively recovers the product-anchor corollary and checks dependent versus independent candidate measurements. Those executable tests are proof-obligation guards; they do not replace the analytic proof or establish that a particular natural system obeys the declared multiplicative model.

The chapter does **not** prove that molecular or genomic observations are generally weak. It does not prove that field observations are generally stronger. It does not infer mechanism existence from non-identification. It does not claim every ecological process is log-linear. It does not turn partial identification into point identification. It does not estimate the correct calibration bound from the same data whose robustness is being assessed.

The safe positive conclusion is sharper than “more data are not always better”:

> **An added observation changes structural mechanism identification exactly when it contributes a new independent direction to the declared observation operator. More precision or more observations along the same span may improve estimation, but they cannot remove an unobserved structural direction.**

## 11. Transition: identification and warning are orthogonal responsibilities

After this chapter, the first shortcut is closed by an exact condition. A measurement earns mechanism-identifying value through its effect on the observation map, not through biological proximity or sheer abundance.

But prediction is a different responsibility. A signal can fail to identify the mechanism that generated it and still forecast an event. Conversely, a detailed mechanistic measurement can be a poor warning if it does not discriminate event from non-event trajectories.

Chapter 2 therefore does not follow as a theorem consequence. It asks an orthogonal question:

> **If a signal consistently appears before ecological loss, what additional denominator condition is required before that precedence becomes predictive discrimination?**

The next chapter restores event and non-event trajectories and proves what perfect precedence fixes—and what it leaves free.
