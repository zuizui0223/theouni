# 一つの系に、状態は一つではない

*English working title: One System Does Not Have One State*

> **Draft status:** source-bounded v0.1 from eco-genetic-criticality snapshot `290663cd25dd2ab06ef8913f97696fd29370f7f2`. The chapter establishes target-dependent state separation in a declared finite eco-genetic closure and a representation boundary from cross-layer alignment. It does not claim that five named states form a universal ontology of nature.

## 1. A single eco-genetic summary can answer the wrong state question

Ecological systems are often summarized by a small number of variables: abundance, occupancy, interaction strength, genetic diversity, trait composition, or a composite state score. Compression is unavoidable. The problem begins when one summary is silently asked to stand for several different scientific responsibilities.

A population may remain present while the interaction environment no longer supports its long-term trait configuration. Genetic diversity may decline without demographic disappearance. Alleles may persist while realised function changes. A model can therefore report one quantity correctly and still fail to answer another state question.

The forbidden inference of this chapter is:

> **生態遺伝的に要約した ⇒ 五つの側面を代表した**

The five target-facing aspects emphasized by the source programme are potential viability, realised occupancy, demographic state, genetic diversity, and allele persistence. They are related through a declared eco-genetic life cycle, but no identity among them is assumed. [G1]

This is not a semantic preference for many state variables. The chapter asks whether those quantities actually respond together under the same fragmentation contrast. If they do not, then a single summary cannot be presumed to represent all five targets.

## 2. Keep theorem, closure, hypothesis, and simulation evidence separate

The eco-genetic programme uses four evidence types. A **Type T** statement is an exact theorem under explicit mathematical assumptions. A **Type C** result follows only after a particular ecological/life-cycle closure is declared. A **Type H** statement is a dynamic hypothesis. A **Type S** result is numerical evidence from a declared finite simulation. [G2]

This taxonomy is essential for this chapter. The claim that one canonical positive-feedback map has a particular fixed-point geometry is not automatically a theorem about the full finite trait–allele simulator. A finite fragmentation response is not automatically a biological law. And a state distinction seen over a 30-generation endpoint is not a universal temporal lag theorem.

The chapter therefore uses exact mathematics only where the source programme has it, and finite simulation evidence only at its own claim level.

## 3. Potential viability is not realised occupancy

The canonical interaction reduction provides one analytical bridge. For a declared positive-feedback map, the source programme derives exact fixed-point conditions and then evaluates a high-trait viability margin on the stable branches. If the high-trait margin is negative on the low interaction branch and positive on the high branch, the high-trait component is potentially unavailable on the low branch and potentially viable on the high branch. [G3]

But potential viability is not realised occupancy. Realised occupancy depends on the finite recruitment, inheritance, dispersal and stochastic closure. A trait distribution already present in the carried state can persist for some time even after the current interaction environment no longer supports the corresponding potential state.

That logical distinction becomes observable in the fragmentation-gradient experiment.

## 4. The fragmentation gradient directly separates state responses

The preregistered fresh-seed sensitivity projected 1,037 independently prepared high-state sources into one, two, three, four, six, eight, twelve, and sixteen equal isolated patches. The source state and projection rules were fixed before examining the gradient outcome. [G4]

The dominant transition occurred immediately at the first subdivision. In the one-patch condition, potential high-trait viability was present in

\[
1037/1037
\]

supported outcomes. At every tested subdivision from two through sixteen patches, potential high-trait viability was absent in

\[
1037/1037
\]

outcomes. [G4]

If potential viability and realised occupancy were interchangeable, realised high-trait occupancy should disappear together with this viability transition. It did not. At the 30-generation endpoint, realised high-trait occupancy persisted in approximately 99.6–100% of supported trajectories across the subdivided conditions. [G4]

This is a direct state-separation result inside the declared finite closure:

> the environment can cross the potential-viability boundary while the carried realised state remains occupied over the finite endpoint.

The conclusion is not that realised occupancy always lags viability by a fixed time. The experiment establishes separation over the tested horizon, not a universal lag law.

## 5. Interaction, effective size, and realised high-trait mass do not share one dose–response shape

The same gradient separates additional response shapes. At two patches, relative to the paired one-patch source, the pooled median retained fractions were approximately

\[
0.001744
\]

for interaction,

\[
0.221311
\]

for local effective size, and

\[
0.282918
\]

for realised high-trait mass. All three quantities were below their paired one-patch values in 1,037/1,037 supported sources. [G4]

But the trajectories then diverged with increasing patch count. Interaction and local effective size continued to decline in every frozen primary cell. Realised high-trait mass instead showed a sharp initial decline followed by partial recovery: the pooled retained fraction was about 0.2829 at two patches, 0.3018 at four patches, and 0.3939 at sixteen patches. [G4]

Thus even quantities that all respond strongly to the first fragmentation step do not collapse to one monotone fragmentation score.

The scientific consequence is target-specific. If the question is whether the interaction environment still supports a potential high-trait component, one state variable is relevant. If the question is whether high-trait individuals are still present at a finite endpoint, another is relevant. If the question is genetic diversity, effective population size, or persistence of a particular allele, yet other summaries may be required.

## 6. Diversity and allele persistence are not demographic synonyms

The finite model also tracks allele frequencies, local diversity, metapopulation diversity, and persistence. The source programme explicitly keeps local diversity \(H_\alpha\), metapopulation diversity \(H_\gamma\), differentiation, and allele persistence distinct rather than treating them as one “genetic state.” [G1]

This matters because deterministic migration bounds, finite drift, mutation, extinction/recolonisation and local census can affect these quantities differently. For example, a row-stochastic migration operator can preserve a common allele-frequency floor under its exact assumptions without proving demographic rescue, trait rescue, or long-run diversity preservation. [G3]

Likewise, the finite fragmentation results can show lower local effective size and changing diversity without implying immediate allele extinction or demographic disappearance.

The chapter therefore treats “genetic condition” as a family of target-dependent outputs rather than one hidden scalar.

## 7. Coarse marginals can agree while the next transition differs

A second, more structural counterexample shows that the problem is not only that several named state variables move at different speeds.

The source programme constructs two explicit simulator states that agree in common coarse summaries—including census, interaction, allele-frequency marginals, realised trait-bin state, \(H_\alpha\), \(H_\gamma\), and \(F_{ST}\)—but differ in patchwise cross-layer alignment. [G5]

Because the ecological transition depends on how trait and genetic components are aligned within patches, the exact next interaction transition can differ between these two states despite agreement in the listed marginals. [G5]

This is a representation boundary. It shows that a summary vector can preserve many familiar eco-genetic marginals and still merge worlds with different next responses.

The long-horizon campaign did **not** establish a directional universal effect of alignment on loss incidence. That negative boundary is important. The construction supports the claim

> matching coarse marginals need not guarantee transition equivalence,

not the stronger claim

> one particular alignment pattern universally causes greater ecological loss.

## 8. Complete simulator state is sufficient under closure, but not automatically minimal

Under the declared simulator closure, the complete explicit present state together with future forcing and the stochastic law is Markov/future-sufficient. [G5]

That statement is mathematically useful but scientifically easy to misuse. A complete simulator state contains enough information by construction, but it can contain many coordinates irrelevant to a particular target. Sufficiency does not imply minimality, and simulator-state identity does not imply scientific-state identity.

This is where TU-3 enters.

## 9. TU-3: raw representation detail and loss-relevant state are different objects

TU-3 formalizes representation invariance relative to a declared loss-response signature. Let representation \(A\) have carrier \(\Omega_A\) and loss-response signature

\[
\sigma_A:\Omega_A\to\Sigma_L.
\]

The loss-state quotient merges exactly those raw states with the same declared loss response.

Now let a richer representation \(B\) map onto \(A\) through a surjection

\[
\pi:\Omega_B\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_A.
\]

If

\[
\sigma_B=\sigma_A\circ\pi,
\]

then \(\pi\) is loss-faithful and induces a canonical bijection between the two loss-state quotients. Raw representation \(B\) may contain many more coordinates while the required loss-state complexity remains unchanged. [T3]

TU-3 makes this sharp with nuisance inflation. For every \(m\ge1\), one can enlarge the raw carrier to

\[
\Omega_B=\Omega_A\times\{0,1\}^m
\]

while keeping the loss signature independent of the added coordinates. Raw state count grows by \(2^m\), but loss-state class count is unchanged. [T3]

So the implication

> more detailed simulator state ⇒ more scientifically required state

is invalid without a target-response argument.

## 10. One hidden response-relevant coordinate is enough to break a coarse state

TU-3 also gives the converse boundary. If two raw states lie in the same projection fiber but have different declared loss-response signatures, then no coarse loss signature can factor through that projection. [T3]

In other words, a coordinate is safely ignorable only while it is nuisance relative to the declared responsibility. Once the coordinate changes the required future response, the coarse representation becomes structurally insufficient.

The cross-layer alignment example is an ecological realization of this logic. Alignment is not universally “part of the state.” It is required when forgetting it merges states that differ in the response the task asks us to preserve.

This is why the chapter title should not be read as metaphysical pluralism. “One system does not have one state” means that scientific state is indexed by the response responsibility. Different targets can require different quotients of the same underlying model world.

## 11. What this chapter establishes—and what it does not

The chapter establishes, within the declared finite eco-genetic programme, that:

1. potential viability and realised occupancy are distinct state questions;
2. the fresh fragmentation gradient produces a direct separation between them;
3. interaction, local effective size, and realised high-trait mass do not share one common fragmentation dose–response shape;
4. demographic, diversity, and allele-persistence targets are kept distinct by the model architecture;
5. common eco-genetic marginals can match while an exact next transition differs because of hidden cross-layer alignment;
6. complete simulator state can be future-sufficient without being the minimal state for every target;
7. TU-3 shows exactly when added raw representation detail is nuisance and when one hidden response-relevant coordinate invalidates a projection.

It does **not** establish that the five named state categories are pairwise different in every configuration. It does not claim they are a complete ontology for natural ecosystems. It does not prove a universal fragmentation threshold, lag law, alignment-risk direction, mutation rate, or demographic consequence. It does not infer natural state adequacy from a simulator theorem. [G6]

The safe conclusion is:

> **An eco-genetic summary earns state status only relative to the response it must preserve; success for one target does not authorize it to stand for potential viability, realised occupancy, demographic state, diversity, and allele persistence simultaneously.**

## 12. Transition: if the required state depends on responsibility, what happens when the future responsibility expands?

Chapter 3 showed that measurement value depends on what ambiguity we are trying to reduce. Chapter 4 now shows that the state itself depends on what response the scientific responsibility requires.

This raises a structural question. Suppose a compact state or interface is adequate under a restricted set of possible futures. What happens when previously forbidden future compositions or interactions become legal? Can the same compact representation remain sufficient merely because the physical boundary of the system is small?

Chapter 5 asks:

> **When the future grammar is opened, how much additional causal memory can be forced even across a narrow physical boundary?**

This transition is an editorial question handoff. The eco-genetic fragmentation result does not prove CCOC's open-grammar memory theorem; CCOC owns a separate finite synthetic extremal construction. [TR]
