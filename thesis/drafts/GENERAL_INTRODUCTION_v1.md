# General Introduction — The Reuse Problem in Ecology

## 1. Ecological science depends on reusing representations

Ecology becomes scientifically and practically usable by compressing heterogeneous systems into reusable representations. Lakes are classified as clear or turbid, populations as viable or declining, communities as functionally intact or impaired, landscapes as connected or fragmented, and trajectories as stable, critical, recovering, or at risk. A reduced state, model, indicator, or warning is valuable precisely because it is not a complete copy of the system. It allows several physically different ecological situations to be treated as equivalent for prediction, explanation, intervention, or reporting.

The difficulty begins when the representation is reused. A state defined for present-status reporting may later be used to choose among restoration actions. A macro-law accepted before turnover may be carried into a community with different members and interactions. A visible state may be treated as mechanism-independent even though retained mechanisms disagree under intervention. An experiment that resolves many latent details may be assumed to license the decision target. A genetic variable that repeatedly changes before functional loss may be called an early warning and transported to another ecological domain. In each case, the original representation may have been adequate for its original task. The failure occurs because adequacy is silently carried across a change in scientific responsibility.

This dissertation studies that reuse problem. Its central question is not whether ecological states are subjective, whether models must simplify, or whether history and hidden mechanisms sometimes matter. Those points are already familiar. The question is sharper:

> **When does a scientific representation that is adequate for one ecological task cease to be reusable after capability, future grammar, structural replacement, mechanism responsibility, evidence, target, representation, or domain changes?**

The answer cannot be supplied by similarity of labels alone. Two tasks may use the same word—state, function, risk, warning—while requiring different distinctions among ecological worlds. Conversely, two representations may look different while preserving exactly the same response needed for a particular task. Reuse is therefore a problem of transport: what scientific work must survive, what was retained, and what map—if any—connects them?

## 2. Adequacy is not the same as indefinite reusability

Let a declared ecological model-world universe be denoted by \(\Omega\). A scientific task \(\alpha\) has a contract-complete response signature

\[
\Sigma_\alpha:\Omega\to Y_\alpha,
\]

which records exactly the distinctions the task requires the representation to preserve. A retained representation is a map

\[
R:\Omega\to Z.
\]

The representation is adequate for \(\alpha\) when there exists a map \(f\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_\alpha=f\circ R.
}
\]

Equivalently, two worlds merged by \(R\) must agree on every response required by \(\alpha\). This elementary factorization is the common grammatical rule of the dissertation. It is not presented as a new mathematical theorem. Its role is to expose what changes from chapter to chapter: the task, the model-world carrier, the response signature, the evidence, the representation already stored, or the domain across which transport is attempted.

Suppose \(R\) was adequate for an earlier task \(\alpha\), but a later task \(\beta\) is introduced. Adequacy for \(\alpha\) does not imply adequacy for \(\beta\). Reuse is licensed only if

\[
\Sigma_\beta=g\circ R
\]

for some map \(g\). If the new task distinguishes two worlds that the old representation merged, no post-processing of the old state label can reconstruct the erased difference. The original representation was not retroactively false. It was task-bounded.

This distinction matters because ecological science routinely treats successful past use as evidence of general portability. A monitoring variable that tracked one intervention outcome is reused when the management repertoire expands. A state aggregation that was exact inside one model is assumed to remain exact after composition. A macro-law is transported after species replacement. A warning statistic that led events in one calibrated domain is reused elsewhere. The dissertation asks, in each case, whether the relevant response still factors through what was retained.

## 3. Eight ways reuse can fail

The research chapters examine eight source-owned failures of transport. They share a typed universe but do not collapse into one theorem.

First, **management capability can outgrow monitoring knowledge**. A new intervention can increase the set of manageable futures by a small amount while exposing arbitrarily many distinctions among present worlds. The scale of the intervention is therefore not a general upper bound on the state resolution or monitoring burden needed to use it.

Second, **closed simplicity can fail under open futures**. Exact interfaces optimized independently under fixed closed response grammars may all be small, while the exact interface required after opening the admissible future grammar becomes much larger. Separate closed compressions do not automatically assemble into an open interface.

Third, **macro-laws can fail under ecological replacement**. When turnover, extinction, recolonization, or rewiring changes the underlying system, an inherited source classification may no longer define an exact target law. The relevant repair is constrained by source semantics: it must preserve every inherited merge that remains valid rather than optimize an unrelated target partition from scratch.

Fourth, **visible equivalence can fail under mechanism uncertainty**. Several mechanisms may produce the same observable present state while implying different responses to a required intervention. A single mechanism-independent law is justified only when all retained response types agree; otherwise typed or set-valued reporting is the honest result.

Fifth, **evidence can fail to license the state or target that theory requires**. An experiment supplies only the distinctions encoded by its reliable records. More observations or a finer nominal record do not help if they resolve target-irrelevant details, share a common failure domain, or leave different target values compatible with the same evidence.

Sixth, **causal learning can diverge from decision licensing**. An observation may substantially reduce uncertainty among causal programmes without resolving the requested management target. Another observation may license the target while teaching little about the causal programme. Information value is always value about a typed object.

Seventh, **raw model detail and familiar eco-genetic summaries can both misrepresent functional-loss state**. A complete simulator state may be sufficient under the simulator's Markov closure but unnecessarily detailed for a loss target. At the same time, census, interaction, trait, and genetic marginals can be too coarse when they erase transition-relevant cross-layer spatial alignment.

Eighth, **temporal precedence can fail as warning**. A genetic signal may precede every observed loss event and replicate under fresh stochastic ensembles, yet fail discrimination if it also fires in every non-event. Within-state ordering, predictive warning validity, numerical threshold portability, and cross-state portability are different claims.

The chapters are ordered to expose these non-obvious prohibitions, not because the theory universe has one necessary narrative sequence. The underlying architecture is a directed dependency structure with many valid traversals. The principal hard dependency is that the loss-generating domain must be fixed without inspecting warning outcomes before warning validity is evaluated.

## 4. Invariants across scientific worldlines

Although the chapter-specific states and observables differ, seven rules remain invariant across the dissertation.

**Reality is not ModelWorld.** A theorem on a declared finite carrier is not automatically a theorem about nature. Empirical use requires an explicit measurement, model-support, validation, and claim-ceiling bridge.

**State is task-indexed.** No repository-local object called a state becomes the intrinsic state of an ecosystem merely by being useful or mathematically sufficient in one model.

**Merging requires response preservation.** Two worlds may share one state only while the response required by the task is constant across their merged class.

**Evidence does not create structural necessity.** Evidence may identify a distinction or license a report. It does not determine by itself which distinction the scientific task requires.

**Required, identified, and reportable remain distinct.** A state can be required but not identified. A full state can remain unidentified while a coarser target is still reportable. When the target varies across compatible worlds, ambiguity is the correct result.

**No bridge is not a contradiction.** Two source programmes may ask orthogonal questions or live on unmatched carriers. Failure to transport a result between them does not create a logical conflict.

**Validity here does not imply portability elsewhere.** Reuse requires an explicit factorization, common carrier, faithful lift, replacement relation, evidence projection, causal-response map, or cross-domain correspondence appropriate to the attempted transport.

These rules prevent two opposite failures. Without task indexing, distinct scientific objects are collapsed under the word state. Without bridge requirements, task relativity can become an escape from criticism: every disagreement is dismissed as a different perspective. The theory universe is useful only if it states positive transport conditions and identifiable failure modes.

## 5. What counts as resolution, and what counts as failure?

A scientific worldline need not end in full identification to end legitimately. It can close with a resolved state; with **licensed ignorance**, where the full state remains unknown but the requested target is constant across the evidence class; with **honest ambiguity**, where the sharp report remains set-valued; with **bounded non-portability**, where a result is valid inside one domain but fails to travel; or as an explicitly open problem because a bridge or adequacy condition is missing.

These outcomes are not the same as failure of the theory universe. Stronger breakdowns occur when the joint carrier is empty or inadequate, the task is post-hoc or non-testable, the required response does not factor through the retained representation, the ModelWorld universe has not earned empirical adequacy, or several modules that are pairwise compatible cannot be jointly realized on one carrier. The present programme has a complete typed pairwise audit but not a global satisfiability theorem. That claim ceiling is part of the result, not an inconvenience to be hidden.

## 6. Dissertation structure and contribution

Part I asks why doing more can require knowing more. CREST establishes a conservation-capability versus state-resolution no-bound result; CCOC isolates the closed-to-open interface structure beneath it.

Part II asks when ecological laws fail to travel. MLTR treats inherited macro-law transport and minimal source-relative repair after replacement; MRM treats law reporting under unresolved response mechanisms.

Part III asks why more information need not provide the needed answer. CED separates experiment-induced information from target licensing under failure and risk contracts; RACH retains causal multiplicity and selects the next observation, while TU-2 prevents causal-learning value from being confused with report licensing.

Part IV asks when an early signal is not a warning. Eco-genetic criticality identifies the boundary between complete simulator state, coarse summaries, and loss-generating state; the warning extension then shows that perfectly repeated event-conditional lead ordering can coexist with complete predictive failure on the full denominator.

The General Synthesis returns to revision after compression. Once science has stored only an old state label, a later task can be recovered from it only if the revised response factors through what was retained. Adequacy has no privileged direction of travel. A representation can be sufficient for a stronger task and therefore reusable for a weaker one, incomparable across tasks, or irreversibly too coarse for a later refinement.

The dissertation's contribution is therefore not a universal ecological state, a catalogue of reasons that snapshots fail, or a claim that every source result is one theorem. It is a source-grounded theory of scientific transport: **which ecological representations travel, which do not, what bridge would be required, and what must be reported when the bridge fails.**
