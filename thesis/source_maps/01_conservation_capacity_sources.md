## C. Restoration-monitoring budgeting connection

### U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (2002)

**Source:** *Case Study Analysis for the Proposed Section 316(b) Phase II Existing Facilities Rule, Parts H–I*.

Supports the factual statement that one official wetland-restoration valuation case study derived monitoring as five percent of the combined land acquisition, transaction, restoration action, and maintenance cost components.

Does not support:

- a general EPA rule for all restoration projects;
- an EPA claim that five percent guarantees scientific adequacy;
- an inference that cost scaling is always inappropriate.

### National Academies (2017)

**Source:** *Effective Monitoring to Evaluate Ecological Restoration in the Gulf of Mexico*, Chapter 3.

Supports the reported historical review in which restoration monitoring averaged thirteen percent of total project cost and ranged from three to sixty-seven percent.

Does not support:

- the CREST no-bound theorem;
- a causal explanation of the variation;
- a universal optimal monitoring fraction.

### Chapter-level inference

The defensible connection is:

> cost- or scale-based budgeting heuristics exist and can be administratively useful, but neither a fixed percentage nor intervention scale alone supplies an epistemic guarantee that monitoring resolves the distinctions required by a new action repertoire.

The chapter must not claim that agencies explicitly endorsed the forbidden mathematical inference.

## E. Formal prior-art boundary

The chapter cites:

- Littman et al. (2002) and Singh et al. (2004) for predictive state representations;
- Nicol and Chadès (2012) and Chadès et al. (2021) for partially observable conservation decision problems;
- Beckers and Halpern (2019) for causal abstraction;
- Konidaris (2019) for action-sensitive abstraction;
- Parker (2020) for adequacy-for-purpose.

These sources support the boundary that action-sensitive state and purpose-relative adequacy are established ideas. CREST's chapter-level result is the connected scale separation among carrier gain, state complexity, evidence debt, and target reportability.
