# Source map — General Introduction v0.2

This map supports `thesis/drafts/00_general_introduction_v0.2.md`. It distinguishes literature-backed background, source-owned chapter previews, and dissertation-level synthesis claims.

## A. Philosophy of representation and purpose

### Odenbaugh (2005, 2019)

Supports the claim that ecological models may be idealized and should be evaluated pragmatically in relation to scientific work rather than by literal resemblance alone.

Does not by itself support the dissertation's cross-task reuse theorem or any ecological state quotient.

### Potochnik (2015, 2017, 2020)

Supports plural scientific aims and the role of idealization relative to those aims.

Does not establish the source-owned transport failures of Chapters 1–8.

### Parker (2020); Bokulich and Parker (2021)

Support adequacy-for-purpose and the distinction between representation and the purpose for which it is evaluated.

The dissertation adds a narrower organizational question: whether a representation adequate for one responsibility may be reused for a later responsibility. This is an interpretation and programme framing, not a claim that prior work never discusses reuse or transfer.

### Batterman (2000); Wimsatt (2007)

Support the background discussion of multiple realization, higher-level regularities, and selective representation.

They do not license the claim that all ecological state differences are micro/macro relations of the same kind.

## B. Ecological model and decision context

### Stringham et al. (2003)

Supports the statement that State-and-Transition Models connect ecological states to management-relevant processes and transitions.

### Nicol and Chadès (2012); Chadès et al. (2021)

Support the statement that conservation POMDPs require decisions about which states and state resolution matter under partial observability.

### Getz et al. (2018)

Supports the statement that ecological model adequacy requires explicit scrutiny of variables, controls, and intended use.

### Yates et al. (2018)

Supports the statement that transferability of ecological models to novel conditions is a recognized challenge.

None of these sources establishes the exact theorem or bridge structure of `theouni`; they motivate the scientific problem.
