## Subject

**Opinion proposal — More measurement is not more evidence: four failures of monotonic reasoning in ecology**

## Body

Dear Editors,

We would like to propose an Opinion for *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* entitled **“More measurement is not more evidence: four failures of monotonic reasoning in ecology.”**

Ecology is becoming measurement-rich, yet several mature literatures show that different forms of “more” do not guarantee stronger evidence. Structural-identifiability theory shows that greater precision need not add a new identification direction; targeted experimental design and ecological value-of-information show that more latent-state information need not be more useful for the declared target; pseudoreplication shows that more observations need not mean more independent evidence; and imperfect-detection, calibration and dataset-shift work show that better downstream classification does not necessarily repair observation-process failures.

Our proposed Opinion does not claim novelty for those component literatures. Its contribution is to organize them into **four diagnostically distinct failures of monotonic reasoning — geometry, objective, dependence and pipeline — and to show that each requires a different measurement-design remedy.** The central practical question becomes not “how can we measure more?” but “what distinction must survive for this scientific responsibility, and what kind of additional measurement can actually change it?”

We would develop the argument with two ecological worked examples. A Campanula island-cline example illustrates how targeted measurements can separate competing mechanism structures that remain compatible with the same coarse ecological pattern. An island-pollination example translates different scientific questions into different required measurement contracts. Both are presented as worked design examples rather than as new field-validation claims.

We think the framework will be relevant across field sampling, sensor networks, experimental ecology, mechanistic inference, biodiversity monitoring and adaptive management. A proposed main figure would contrast the four failure modes, and two short boxes would translate the framework into ecological measurement decisions.

We have prepared a concise proposal, an external prior-art map, and a source/claim firewall that keeps the component methods and results with their original papers. We would be grateful to know whether this concept could be suitable for an Opinion in *TREE* before developing a full manuscript.
