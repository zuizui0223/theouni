# Editor-facing pitch — Trends in Ecology & Evolution

## Proposed Opinion

**When more measurement is not more evidence**

Ecologists and evolutionary biologists can now measure more often, more precisely and with increasingly automated tools. Yet the familiar metrics used to justify more measurement are **local quantities conditioned on earlier choices and losses**. Precision is conditional on the observational axis already chosen. Information gain is conditional on which parts of the system count in the objective. Replicate count is conditional on inferential unit and failure architecture. Classifier accuracy is conditional on records that survived upstream sampling and processing.

We propose an Opinion arguing that this shared conditional structure explains why more measurement can fail to become more evidence for a declared ecological or evolutionary claim. The relevant distinction must be **preserved**, the retained measurement must **separate** live alternatives, that separation must be **relevant** to the declared target, and repeated support must contain enough **failure diversity** not to depend on one common point of failure.

This is not another warning that “more data can be bad,” nor a universal evidence score. It is a **prospective diagnostic doctrine**: before collecting new data, identify the limiting interface and choose among equal-cost measurement expansions accordingly.

| interface | diagnostic question | failure | matched action |
|---|---|---|---|
| timely preservation | is the needed distinction still present in the retained record? | too late | capture earlier or add an independent observation path |
| separation | does the measurement distinguish a live alternative? | same dimension | measure a new distinction |
| relevance | can that distinction change the declared prediction, contrast or decision? | wrong target | measure target-relevant information |
| failure diversity | does support depend on one shared failure domain? | false independence | diversify failure domains |

The interfaces interact. A second sensor may improve failure diversity while introducing a new detection or calibration problem; primer diversity may reduce one blind spot while reducing depth per primer; a target-specific measurement may improve relevance while sacrificing broader mechanism information. The paper therefore treats measurement design as the management of a **flow of distinctions**, not four independent warnings.

## What is new

The component ideas are deliberately not claimed as new. Multiple working hypotheses and strong inference already make discrimination among alternatives central to science (Chamberlin 1890; Platt 1964). Structural-identifiability and ecological parameter-redundancy work distinguish estimability from precision (Villaverde et al. 2016; Catchpole et al. 1996; Cole & McCrea 2016). Targeted/goal-oriented design and ecological value-of-information analysis make information value objective-dependent (Vanlier et al. 2012; Attia et al. 2018; Canessa et al. 2015). Pseudoreplication and modern design distinguish nominal sample count from independent replication (Hurlbert 1984; Marshall 2024). Imperfect-detection, calibration, ecological AI and metabarcoding literatures show how downstream outputs inherit upstream observation constraints (MacKenzie et al. 2002; Lahoz-Monfort et al. 2014; Dormann 2020; Alberdi et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2025; Cowans et al. 2026). Data-intensive ecology and recent TREE work also make clear that large observational data and experiments are complementary rather than substitutes (Michener & Jones 2012; McCleery et al. 2023).

The proposed synthesis is instead:

> **Local performance metrics become adequate evidence proxies only when the conditions on which they are calculated preserve and create the distinction required by the declared claim.**

That common structure generates a falsifiable prediction. Given a predeclared claim and several equal-cost measurement expansions, **diagnosing the limiting interface before collecting new data should predict which intervention produces the largest gain in claim resolution**. This can be tested with known-truth simulations, monitoring systems with reference channels or audit samples, and prospective equal-effort field/sensor comparisons.

## Why now

Ecological inference increasingly combines automated sensors, remote sensing, eDNA, genomic assays, classifier outputs and multistage digital pipelines. These systems make it easy to improve a downstream local metric while leaving the decisive scientific distinction unresolved upstream. The gap between measurement volume and claim resolution is therefore becoming a general design problem rather than a niche statistical concern.

## Worked examples

**Island *Campanula*.** Rather than assuming one floral trait uniquely diagnoses pollinator selection, the worked design asks for decoupled contrasts: island/site pairs overlapping in climate and elevation but differing in pollinator community, or pollinator exclusions that change visitation while holding the abiotic setting fixed. Visitor identity, effective pollen transfer and reproductive consequence then provide a mechanism-separating observation package. This is a prospective worked design example, not a new empirical-validation claim.

**Metabarcoding.** Deeper sequencing or a better classifier helps only if the focal sequence survived field sampling, extraction and amplification. If the record survives but calibration semantics drift, downstream recalibration may help; if the sequence never entered the retained pool, intervention must move upstream. Replication across extraction/primer/sampling modes can also matter more than deeper sequencing within one shared failure domain.

## Practical message

> **Before collecting more data, ask what distinction the next measurement must create or preserve for the conclusion to change.**

Then test the doctrine prospectively: did the diagnosis identify the equal-cost measurement expansion that actually helped most?

- **Figure 1:** biological opportunity → retained record → distinguishable alternatives → declared target, with timely preservation, separation and relevance on the chain and failure diversity cross-cutting observation paths.

## Relationship to the Boundary Perspective proposal

The Boundary proposal asks the narrower question of whether mechanistic proximity and identification strength are distinct axes. If both routes proceed, the **Boundary paper retains the full identification theorem** and Γ–κ development; the TREE Opinion states only that separation can have formal, testable conditions and uses the qualitative **same-dimension/separation lesson** within the broader measurement-to-evidence synthesis.
