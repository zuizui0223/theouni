## Short request

Subject: **Could you sanity-check a short TREE Opinion pitch?**

Hi [Name],

I am preparing a short presubmission proposal for a *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* Opinion titled **“When more measurement is not more evidence.”**

Before sending it to the editor, I would really value a broad-interest ecological read from someone who is not already inside the theory/methods work behind it. I am not asking you to check proofs or code. I mainly want to know whether the ecological point is clear and whether you recover one common argument rather than four familiar warnings placed side by side.

The central claim is that familiar performance metrics are **local quantities conditioned on earlier measurement choices and losses**. Higher precision, more information, more replicates or better classifier accuracy become useful evidence only when those conditions preserve or create the distinction required by the declared ecological or evolutionary claim.

I also want to know whether the paper's **prospective intervention-ranking prediction** is understandable: given a predeclared claim and several equal-cost measurement expansions, diagnosing the limiting interface before collecting new data should predict which intervention produces the largest gain in claim resolution.

The read is deliberately small: please look only at the send-candidate text and the short editor pitch, then answer the questions in the one-page reader packet. A useful answer can be very brief; the most valuable thing is your **first concrete reason to reject or stop reading**.

Thank you — even a short critical read would be extremely helpful.

Best,
Ruiqi
