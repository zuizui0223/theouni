Subject: **Opinion proposal — When more measurement is not more evidence**

Dear Editors,

We would like to propose an Opinion for *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* entitled **“When more measurement is not more evidence.”**

Ecologists and evolutionary biologists can now measure more often, more precisely and with increasingly automated tools. Mature literatures already show why design matters: structural identification separates estimability from precision, value-of-information and optimal-design methods make information target-dependent, replication depends on inferential structure, and observation-process models show how upstream sampling shapes downstream inference. Most directly, the recent TREE **LIES of omission** framework already provides a cross-field typology of ecological observation problems. Our proposed Opinion is therefore **not another observation-process taxonomy**.

The narrower question is prospective: **for a declared ecological or evolutionary claim, what class of measurement must change next?** We organize that choice around four interfaces—**timely preservation, separation, relevance and failure diversity**—and treat them as a remedy-matched intervention screen. The practical question changes from “how can we measure more?” to **“what distinction must the next measurement create or preserve for the conclusion to change?”**

When separation is limiting, the next measurement should be one that **separates an unresolved explanation**, not merely the old quantity more precisely. When relevance is limiting, the study needs information that can change its declared prediction, contrast or decision. When shared failure is limiting, another nominal replicate is less valuable than another independent failure opportunity. When preservation is limiting, the distinction must be captured earlier in the observation pipeline, or recalibrated only if it remains encoded in the retained record.

This framing makes a falsifiable measurement-choice prediction. Given a predeclared claim and several equal-cost measurement expansions, diagnosis of the limiting interface should predict whether effort is better spent on earlier capture, a new separating axis, target-relevant information or a new failure domain. We do not claim that this replaces a correctly specified full value-of-information or optimal-design model. Its proposed value is to identify the missing **intervention class**, including upstream or shared-failure bottlenecks that a downstream performance metric can hide.

We would develop the argument with two worked examples. In an island *Campanula* design, climate/elevation and pollinator effects are separated prospectively using decoupled site contrasts or pollinator exclusions, followed by visitor identity, effective pollen transfer and reproductive consequence. In metabarcoding, deeper sequencing or a better classifier helps only if the focal taxon survived earlier stages such as field sampling, extraction and amplification; when loss occurs upstream, the intervention must also move upstream. Both examples are worked design illustrations rather than new empirical-validation claims.

We think this claim-specific measurement-choice doctrine could be useful across field sampling, biodiversity monitoring, sensor networks, experimental ecology, evolutionary inference and adaptive management. We have prepared a concise proposal, a validated full Opinion draft for downstream use if invited, and a prior-art map that explicitly positions the argument against LIES, sampling-design/inference theory, structural identifiability and value-of-information/optimal-design work.

We would be grateful to know whether this concept could be suitable for an Opinion in *TREE* before submitting the full manuscript.
